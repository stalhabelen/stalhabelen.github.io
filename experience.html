Space Defense Technologies – SDT

Applied research internship · 2025

Focused on bringing neural rendering ideas into seismic subsurface imaging, designing a novel architecture that bridges NeRF-style volumetric rendering with marine seismic acquisition for faster and more data-efficient interpretation.

  • Neural Rendering for Seismic Imaging. Implemented a compact Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) pipeline from scratch in PyTorch, using COLMAP for structure-from-motion. Adapted the core idea of multi-view consistency and volumetric rendering to seismic CMP gathers: instead of camera poses, offset and azimuth serve as the "view" parameters, and the network learns to predict subsurface reflectivity as a continuous neural field.
  • Proposed Architecture: Tokenized Seismic Rendering. Designed an end-to-end pipeline where seismic traces are first transformed into time–frequency representations (spectrograms / scalograms), then discretized into compact token sequences via vector quantization. These tokens feed into a transformer-based decoder that reconstructs CMP-level subsurface structure. The architecture treats each seismic gather as a "scene" and learns cross-offset consistency through attention, analogous to how NeRF learns cross-view consistency in 3D rendering. This approach enables the model to generalize across acquisition geometries and handle sparse or irregular sampling.
  • Audio Tokenization as a Foundation. The tokenization stage of this pipeline directly builds on my Multimodal Audio Tokenizer project, which explores how continuous waveforms can be converted into discrete, reusable token representations. The same vector-quantized encoding and codebook learning techniques developed for audio signals transfer naturally to seismic traces, since both are 1-D time-series with rich spectral structure. This connection between audio and seismic tokenization is a key insight of the proposed architecture.
  • Research Directions. Exploring physics-informed priors (wave-equation constraints) to regularize the neural field, and investigating how self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled seismic data can improve generalization with limited labeled surveys.
