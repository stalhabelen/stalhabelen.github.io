About

Hi, I'm Talha — an EEE student at Bilkent University working at the intersection of robot learning, computer vision, and optimization. I'm interested in how robots can perceive 3D scenes, reason about objects and tools, and plan meaningful physical actions in the real world.

Most of my work focuses on dexterous robotic manipulation, world models, and grounding visual predictions in physical interaction through contact inference and real-robot rollouts. Right now I'm doing research on affordance-guided world models for dexterous manipulation at NYU CILVR with Lerrel Pinto, and contributing to CVXPY at Stanford on disciplined nonlinear programming.

I've studied as a visiting student at UC Berkeley and Stanford, taking courses in machine learning, convex optimization, information devices and systems, and technology leadership. Outside of coursework, I like building projects that tie together perception, planning, and control — with the long-term goal of making robotic systems more capable and reliable.

DexRefineRGB-guided, contact-aware robot reference refinement

Dexterous Manipulation · Human-to-Robot Retargeting · Contact Guidance · Physics Optimization
DexRefine turns noisy human hand–object demonstrations into physically plausible robot references using RGB contact guidance, grasp-locked correction, Wuji retargeting, collision-aware refinement, and SPIDER/MJWP physics rollouts.

ToolMindVLM-guided spatial reasoning for robotic tool use

Best Project · Learning for Robotics · VLMs · Robot Tool Use
We developed a structured VLM Chain-of-Thought method for geometry analysis, grasp localization, and contact-tip localization, followed by depth refinement and KOMO motion planning. Selected as the Best Project in Bilkent University's Learning for Robotics course.
